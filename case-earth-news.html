History and the politics of memory

I currently work as an Associate Researcher at The Earth News in Jammu, India. This ongoing research examines competing interpretations of Gulab Singh and the founding of the Dogra state, with a focus on the relationship between historical evidence, moral judgement, and political memory.

The central question is how accounts of nineteenth-century rule acquire new meanings in later debates about legitimacy, identity, and belonging.

Five areas of enquiry

  • Gulab Singh’s role in the First Anglo-Sikh War.
  • Interpretations of the Treaty of Amritsar of 1846.
  • The fiscal regime and communal exemptions.
  • Begar, or forced labour.
  • Taxation and labour in the shawl economy.

Reading interpretations alongside evidence

The draft brings pro-Dogra and critical historiographical traditions into conversation, examining how authors’ contexts, audiences, and political settings shape their readings of the past.

It considers treaties, historical accounts, and subsequent scholarship alongside the later public uses of Dogra history, including debates around 1947 and the constitutional changes of 2019.
